LGR TRANSIENT SIMULATOR - DEVELOPMENT NOTES
November 2026

OVERVIEW
An experimental transient model has been added alongside the existing quasi-static (QSS) lap time simulator. The QSS solver still provides a target speed profile; the new model simulates how the car responds over time while attempting to follow that profile and the racing line.

WHAT HAS BEEN ADDED

Transient vehicle model (transientDynamics.py)
- VehicleState tracks longitudinal/lateral velocity, yaw rate, X/Y position and heading between timesteps.
- A simplified dynamic bicycle model calculates tyre slip angles and forces, then updates the car's motion using fixed timesteps.
- Throttle, braking, powertrain forces and aerodynamic drag determine longitudinal acceleration.
- A simplified friction ellipse limits combined braking/acceleration and cornering forces, rather than treating grip in each direction independently.

Controls and circuit integration
- A Pure Pursuit controller steers towards a lookahead point on the existing racing line.
- Current track progress is estimated by projecting the vehicle position onto the reference path.
- Predictive throttle and braking controllers simulate several candidate inputs over short horizons and choose inputs using provisional speed, stability and path-following criteria. They are currently separate controllers.

Outputs
- Trajectory plot comparing the reference circuit against the simulated car.
- Speed (actual versus QSS target), throttle/brake and path-error traces, plus yaw-rate and replay-time outputs.

CURRENT STATUS / ASSUMPTIONS
- The car can follow much of the FSUK circuit, but sometimes cuts corners or deviates from the path, and speed control is not yet consistent.
- A recent replay reported about 74.33 s versus 70.07 s from QSS. This is NOT yet a validated lap time: completion uses estimated reference-track progress, not a verified finish-line crossing within track boundaries.
- The model uses simplified planar dynamics, an estimated yaw inertia (100 kg m^2), approximate combined tyre grip and no detailed suspension or transient tyre relaxation. Behaviour during major spins or at very low forward speeds is unreliable.

NEXT STEPS
1. Combine throttle, coasting and braking into one predictive controller; investigate unnecessary braking and missed target speeds.
2. Check the rapidly fluctuating QSS target speeds against track curvature.
3. Improve steering/path following and implement reliable track-boundary and finish-line checks.
4. Validate vehicle parameters and dynamics before attempting lap-time optimisation.
